Retrotransposition insertion types

Shared reference for the PEAR-TREE caller, the simulators and annotation — what each insertion type looks like in the genome and in short reads, how often it occurs, and how PEAR-TREE names and scores it. Branch tprt-hallmarks; vocabulary per plans/tprt_hallmarks/SPEC.md.

Vocabulary and evidence

TPRT hallmarks
  • TSD — target-site duplication, typically 4–25 bp (in vivo average ~14 bp), A-rich.
  • EN motif — the L1 endonuclease nick, consensus 5′-TTTT/AA-3′ (a preference, not a fixed sequence: even the most used single 7-mer, TTTTT/AA, accounts for < 10% of insertions).
  • poly(A) at the 3′ end, on the strand consistent with element orientation (poly(T) when the element is on the minus strand).
  • 5′ truncation / inversion as products of early termination and twin priming.
PEAR-TREE output (SPEC)
  • element: L1, ALU, SVA, PSEUDOGENE, POLYA_ONLY, ORPHAN_TD, NON_TPRT, UNKNOWN
  • structure: FULL_LENGTH, TRUNCATED_5P, INVERTED_5P, INVERTED_5P_SWITCH, 5P_UNRESOLVED
  • tags: TD3P, TD5P, TD3P_SOURCE=…, NOVEL_SOURCE, TEMPLATED_LOCAL, PREMRNA_COINSERT, TSD_DELETION, EN_INDEPENDENT, L1_MED_DELETION, L1_MED_DUPLICATION, FOLDBACK_INVDUP_5P, CHIMERIC_ENDS, EXON_JUNCTION
  • evidence roles: CLIP, POLYA, MATE, DISC, SPAN; a junction is supported with ≥ 2 independent fragments pooled over all colonies of a patient — the poly-A end included; independence is decided from the reads, never from the duplicate flag.
  • tprt_call: TPRT / LIKELY_TPRT / UNCERTAIN / ARTEFACT_LIKE from the additive tprt_score.

1Solo-L1 (full-length, 5′-truncated, 5′-inverted) in scope

An L1 RNA is reverse-transcribed at its own insertion site by target-primed reverse transcription (TPRT): the L1 endonuclease nicks the bottom strand at a T-rich motif (consensus 5′-TTTT/AA-3′), the exposed 3′-OH primes cDNA synthesis on the RNA poly(A) tail, and a staggered second nick creates the target-site duplication (TSD). Because reverse transcription usually stops early, almost all somatic copies keep only the 3′ end of L1 (5′ truncation). In twin priming the second DNA end of the break anneals internally on the same RNA and primes a second cDNA, so the 5′ part is inverted relative to the 3′ part; at the inversion point a short internal deletion (poly-dT cDNA stopped short of the internal primer) or duplication (it ran past it) is common.

Reported frequency

Zumalave 2026 (10 high-rate tumours, 6,418 events): 56% of all events (3,611); of 5′-truncated solo-L1s 49% 5′-deletion only and 51% 5′-inverted; full-length only 0.3% (10/3,611). Of inverted copies 66% carry an internal deletion (median 14.5 bp), 17% an internal duplication (median 22 bp). PCAWG: 14,967/19,166 (78%). Nam 2023 normal colon: 89% solo-L1 (1,063/1,198), 29.5% with a short 5′ inversion. Tubio 2014: ~5% of non-inverted cancer L1s full-length.

How PEAR-TREE detects it

  • discovery: CLIP reads at both junctions; POLYA reads placed by their mate on the 3′ side; DISC anchors whose mates hit L1
  • annotate: element L1; structure from the 5′ clip consensus — FULL_LENGTH (clip reaches L1 5′ UTR start), TRUNCATED_5P, INVERTED_5P (5′ clip/mates on the opposite strand), else 5P_UNRESOLVED
  • score: TSD 4–25 bp with identity; poly-A ≥ 10 on the strand-consistent side; EN motif at the nick; 5′/3′ class concordant; identity to an active L1HS; twin-priming junction ≥ 590 bp into L1; ≥ 2 independent fragments per junction pooled over colonies

Vocabulary: element=L1; structure=FULL_LENGTH | TRUNCATED_5P | INVERTED_5P | 5P_UNRESOLVED

2Partnered 3′ transduction in scope

Transcription of a source L1 reads through its weak polyadenylation signal and stops at a downstream site, so the RNA carries unique flanking sequence after the L1 3′ end. The new copy therefore reads L1 → (source poly-A, often short) → source 3′ flank → poly-A. The transduced segment is a barcode for the source element. Zumalave et al. showed that the strength of the canonical vs. downstream polyadenylation sites of a source decides whether it makes mostly solo-L1s or mostly transductions.

Reported frequency

Zumalave 2026: 1,535 partnered (24% of 6,418). Tubio 2014: transductions 24% of somatic L1s (655/2,756), about half partnered. Nam 2023 normal colon: only 1% (11/1,198). PCAWG: 3,669 transductions (partnered + orphan, 19%). Transduced segments reach 12 kb but are mostly short.

How PEAR-TREE detects it

  • discovery: the 3′ CLIP/POLYA reads carry poly-A then non-L1 sequence; DISC mates on the 3′ side map with high MAPQ to another chromosome (translocation-like)
  • combine: indel-aware consensus must keep the sequence beyond the poly-A (beyond_polya)
  • annotate: element L1, tag TD3P + TD3P_SOURCE=<id> from flanks_3p.fa, or NOVEL_SOURCE under the novel-source rule
  • score: covered 3′-beyond-poly-A sequence (extra points with ≥ 2 independent fragments); the 3′ tag being the transduced flank of exactly the element at the 5′ end counts as concordant

Vocabulary: element=L1; tags=TD3P,TD3P_SOURCE=…

3Orphan 3′ transduction in scope

The same read-through transcript, but reverse transcription is so 5′-truncated that no L1 sequence is copied: only unique downstream sequence of a source plus a poly-A lands at the new site. With short reads it looks like a small unique-sequence insertion or a fake translocation to the source locus; the poly-A, TSD and EN motif are what identify it as retrotransposition.

Reported frequency

Zumalave 2026: 705 (11% of 6,418). Tubio 2014: 333/655 transductions (≈12% of all somatic L1). Nam 2023 normal colon: 124/1,198 (10%) — orphans far outnumber partnered events there.

How PEAR-TREE detects it

  • discovery: CLIP reads whose clips map uniquely elsewhere (not to a repeat) — must not be dropped by an element filter; POLYA reads on the 3′ side
  • annotate: element ORPHAN_TD, TD3P_SOURCE=<id>; the segment must lie within 15 kb downstream (strand-aware) of a source L1, otherwise it is not an orphan transduction
  • score: poly-A + TSD + EN motif carry the call because there is no element sequence; concordance = 5′ clip and 3′ tag map to the same source flank

Vocabulary: element=ORPHAN_TD; tags=TD3P_SOURCE=…

4Alu and SVA insertions; SVA 5′ transduction in scope

Alu (~0.3 kb) and SVA (a few kb; hexamer, Alu-like, VNTR and SINE-R modules) are non-autonomous: they are mobilised in trans by L1 ORF2p, so they carry the same TPRT hallmarks (TSD, poly-A, EN motif). An Alu is shorter than a sequencing fragment, so read pairs often span the whole insert. SVAs driven by an upstream cellular promoter can carry 5′ transductions — upstream flank (frequently an exon of the host gene, e.g. the MAST2-driven SVA_F group) placed in front of the SVA.

Reported frequency

Zumalave 2026: Alu 119 (2%), SVA 6 (<0.1%). PCAWG: Alu 130, SVA 23 (together <1%). SVA 5′ transductions: ~8% of all reference SVAs (Damert 2009, germline); no somatic rate available.

How PEAR-TREE detects it

  • discovery: as for L1; SPAN pairs (D2 flag) help very short inserts
  • annotate: element ALU / SVA; tag TD5P when the 5′ clip maps to flanks_5p_sva.fa
  • score: identity to young AluY / SVA_E-F consensus; old subfamily only scores negative

Vocabulary: element=ALU | SVA; tags=TD5P

5Processed pseudogene in scope

L1 ORF2p occasionally reverse-transcribes a spliced cellular mRNA. The new copy has a TSD and a poly-A like an L1 insertion, but its body is exons joined without introns. Discordant mates map to the exons of the source gene, which alone would look like many small deletions at the source; the exon–exon junction read (a read aligned in exon n whose clip is exon n+1) is the proof that the inserted sequence is processed mRNA.

Reported frequency

Zumalave 2026: 133 (2%), up to 31 in one tumour. PCAWG: 274 (1.4%), 70 of them in a single pancreatic tumour. Germline (Ewing 2013): 48 gene retrocopy insertions in 1000 Genomes data, exon–exon junctions recovered for 39.

How PEAR-TREE detects it

  • discovery: CLIP at both insertion junctions with clips that map to a gene; poly-A reads
  • annotate: element PSEUDOGENE + tag EXON_JUNCTION when the consensus/reads.fa contain a junction joining two annotated exons at their splice sites (exon track on hs1)
  • score: exon–exon junction is a positive feature; without it the call stays UNCERTAIN

Vocabulary: element=PSEUDOGENE; tags=EXON_JUNCTION

6Solitary poly(A/T) insertion in scope

An extreme 5′ truncation: reverse transcription copies only the RNA poly(A) tail before the second strand is resolved, leaving a TSD-flanked poly(A) (or poly(T) on the minus strand) with no element sequence. It is the hardest true type because a poly-A clip is also the commonest artefact signature (see poly-A slippage).

Reported frequency

Zumalave 2026: 157 (2% of 6,418). Not reported separately in PCAWG or Nam 2023.

How PEAR-TREE detects it

  • discovery: CLIP reads with A(n) clips on both sides; the single-read poly-A rescue passes single fragments through
  • annotate: element POLYA_ONLY
  • score: requires a real TSD and EN motif plus ≥ 2 independent fragments at each end; negative if the site is an A-rich reference tract (slippage context)

Vocabulary: element=POLYA_ONLY

7L1-mediated deletion in scope

The L1 cDNA, primed at the original target site, pairs with a 3′ overhang of a distant pre-existing break upstream instead of the second nick; aberrant repair removes everything in between. The insertion keeps a poly-A and EN motif at the 3′ end but has no TSD; the 5′ end joins via 1–5 bp microhomology. Short reads show a one-sided cluster (no reciprocal cluster within ~500 bp) plus a copy-number loss.

Reported frequency

PCAWG: 90 events, 0.5 kb – 53.4 Mb; 5′ microhomology (median 3 bp) in 75% (47/63) resolved junctions; EN motif at the 3′ end in 82% (74/90); 8% (7/90) EN-independent. Zumalave 2026: deletion-like junctions are the largest rearrangement class, 43% (66/152).

How PEAR-TREE detects it

  • discovery: two one-sided junctions separated by the deleted segment; may be emitted as two loci
  • annotate: tag L1_MED_DELETION (with element L1 or ORPHAN_TD); 5′ microhomology instead of TSD
  • score: EN motif + poly-A at the 3′ junction; no TSD bonus; tag carried, not treated as an artefact

Vocabulary: tags=L1_MED_DELETION

8L1-mediated duplication, fold-back inversion and BFB initiation in scope

When the L1 cDNA engages a break on the sister chromatid or upstream of the target, the intervening segment is duplicated rather than lost. In the fold-back form an L1 bridges the two sister chromatids in head-to-head orientation: two clusters with the same orientation sit a few kb apart, copy number rises on one side, and the dicentric product can start breakage–fusion–bridge (BFB) cycles that amplify oncogenes (CCND1 in two PCAWG tumours).

Reported frequency

PCAWG: individual cases (79.6 Mb 14q duplication; CCND1 BFB amplification in 2 tumours) among 103 L1-mediated rearrangements. Zumalave 2026: duplication-like 9% (14/152) of rearrangements.

How PEAR-TREE detects it

  • discovery: clusters whose mates hit L1 on both sides with the same orientation
  • annotate: tag L1_MED_DUPLICATION (copy-number context is outside PEAR-TREE; the tag records the junction geometry)
  • score: TPRT hallmarks at the poly-A junction score as usual

Vocabulary: tags=L1_MED_DUPLICATION

9Retrotransposon-mediated rearrangements (deletion-, duplication-, inversion-like) partly in scope

Zumalave et al. define an RT-RG as two distant breakpoints joined by a somatic retrotransposition bridge and classify intrachromosomal ones by breakpoint orientation: deletion-like [+/−], duplication-like [−/+], inversion-like [+/+] or [−/−]; interchromosomal ones are translocation-like. The bridge is most often a solo-L1 but can be any type, including transductions and pseudogenes.

Reported frequency

Zumalave 2026: 152 RT-RGs = 2% of events; deletion-like 43%, interchromosomal 30%, inversion-like 18%, duplication-like 9%; bridges are solo-L1 in 66%. PCAWG: 103 L1-mediated rearrangements, mostly deletions.

How PEAR-TREE detects it

  • deletion-like → L1_MED_DELETION, duplication-like → L1_MED_DUPLICATION (in scope as tags)
  • inversion-like and translocation-like bridges are out of scope this round; they should still come out as single junctions with TPRT hallmarks rather than be scored as artefacts

Vocabulary: tags=L1_MED_DELETION | L1_MED_DUPLICATION (inversion-/translocation-like: not called)

10Reciprocal translocation bridged by two concurrent L1s out of scope this round

Two synchronous L1 insertions on different chromosomes exchange partners during second-strand resolution, so each derivative chromosome carries one complete L1 insertion (own poly-A, own EN motif) and each insertion's two TSD copies end up on different derivatives — a target-site heteroduplication (e.g. 3 bp and −10 bp in one case). Short reads see four one-sided junctions that look like two translocation partners each bridged by an L1.

Reported frequency

Zumalave 2026: 13 reciprocal translocations (20 of 45 interchromosomal bridges); 10/13 from two distinct synchronous insertions, 3/13 from a single event.

How PEAR-TREE detects it

  • out of scope this round: no pairing of junctions across chromosomes
  • each junction may still be reported as a one-sided L1 junction; it must not be penalised as CHIMERIC_ENDS just because the two ends map to different chromosomes

Vocabulary: — (not called)

11Chimeric bridges out of scope this round

In some reciprocal translocations the two bridges are mosaics of two retrotransposition events (e.g. two different partnered transductions) that recombined while the cDNAs were growing. One bridge has both TPRT ends (a poly-A, a poly-T and two EN motifs); its partner contains the leftover L1 pieces with no poly-A and no EN motif. The exchange points coincide with orientation flips, as in twin priming.

Reported frequency

Zumalave 2026: a subset of the 13 reciprocal translocations (example PD0331a, 22p/12q); no separate rate.

How PEAR-TREE detects it

  • out of scope this round
  • a hallmark-free bridge looks like an artefact chimera (CHIMERIC_ENDS); that is acceptable while bridges are out of scope

Vocabulary: — (not called)

12Reciprocal inversions and complex events out of scope this round

The same in-trans second-strand synthesis between two concurrent L1 reactions (one canonical, one twin-primed) can produce paracentric inversions where each breakpoint is bridged by part of the same L1, or chains of rearrangements with acentric/circular products and large deletions when a single twin-primed cDNA repairs two pre-existing breaks.

Reported frequency

Zumalave 2026: rare — e.g. a 2.67 Mb paracentric inversion (PD0307a) and a chromosome-1 complex event with a 30 Mb loss (PD0331a).

How PEAR-TREE detects it

  • out of scope this round

Vocabulary: — (not called)

13Twin priming with 5′ or 3′ switching in scope

Variants of twin priming in which one of the two growing cDNAs changes template on the L1 RNA. In 5′ switching the internally primed cDNA jumps to a region of the RNA not yet copied; in 3′ switching the poly-dT-primed cDNA jumps to a region downstream of the internal primer. Both leave an extra internal inversion or a coordinate discontinuity at the twin-priming junction, on top of the usual 5′ inversion.

Reported frequency

Zumalave 2026: 20 with 5′ switching and 3 with 3′ switching, out of 1,836 5′-inverted solo-L1s (~1%). Szak 2002 had already noted twice-inverted genomic L1s.

How PEAR-TREE detects it

  • requires the covered-element consensus from insertions.reads.fa; usually only resolved when the insert is short enough for reads to cross the extra junctions
  • annotate: structure=INVERTED_5P_SWITCH; falls back to INVERTED_5P / 5P_UNRESOLVED when the extra junction is not covered

Vocabulary: structure=INVERTED_5P_SWITCH

14Templated local insertions (local chimeric L1) in scope

A non-L1 segment is copied into the L1 during integration, giving a chimeric L1 that duplicates a small genomic segment. Zumalave et al. report templates typically shorter than 250 bp lying within 15 bp of the insertion site, and favour a model in which the growing cDNA leaves the L1 RNA and anneals to nearby homologous DNA (akin to synthesis-dependent strand annealing). In reads the extra segment is a clip that maps back to the immediate neighbourhood, followed by L1. Where in the insert the segment sits varies; the figure draws it at the 5′ end.

Reported frequency

Zumalave 2026: 22 events among ~3,600 solo-L1s (<1%).

How PEAR-TREE detects it

  • annotate: tag TEMPLATED_LOCAL when a non-element segment of the insert (typically < 250 bp) maps within 15 bp of the site
  • must not trigger the fold-back artefact penalty: the clip continues into element sequence and the 3′ end carries poly-A + TSD

Vocabulary: tags=TEMPLATED_LOCAL

15Co-inserted local pre-mRNA in scope

The reverse transcriptase switches from the L1 RNA to a pre-mRNA transcribed near the insertion site and co-inserts part of it, introns included. Unlike a processed pseudogene the co-inserted piece is unspliced and comes from a gene next to the site.

Reported frequency

Nam 2023: a single event (in a clone from an adenoma) among ~1,200 resolved insertions.

How PEAR-TREE detects it

  • annotate: tag PREMRNA_COINSERT when the 5′ part of the insert maps to a gene within the neighbourhood and spans an exon–intron boundary (unspliced); distinct from EXON_JUNCTION

Vocabulary: tags=PREMRNA_COINSERT

16Fold-back inverted duplication 5′ of the target site in scope

After integration, extra DNA synthesis at the 5′ end copies a short stretch of upstream flank back on itself, leaving an inverted duplication of 52–220 bp between the 5′ TSD and the L1. Its 5′ clip is therefore the reverse complement of the adjacent reference — the same as a fold-back artefact — but it is followed by L1 and the 3′ end carries a normal poly-A and TSD.

Reported frequency

Nam 2023: 3 events (0.3%) plus 1 combined with a 5′ inversion (0.1%) among ~1,200.

How PEAR-TREE detects it

  • annotate: tag FOLDBACK_INVDUP_5P; the score's “clip identical to the adjacent reference” penalty applies only when the clip does not continue into element sequence and there is no TPRT 3′ end

Vocabulary: tags=FOLDBACK_INVDUP_5P

ATarget-site deletion instead of a TSD in scope

If the second nick lies upstream of the first, or the overhang is trimmed, the insertion is flanked by a small deletion of the target rather than a duplication. The 5′ and 3′ junction positions are then separated by the deleted bases instead of overlapping.

Reported frequency

Common enough that Nam 2023 plot negative target-site lengths alongside TSDs and Zumalave call it fairly frequent; neither source gives a fraction in the text.

How PEAR-TREE detects it

  • annotate: tag TSD_DELETION, tsd_len negative
  • score: weak positive for deletions ≤ 20 bp (strong positive is reserved for TSD 4–25 bp with identity)

Vocabulary: tags=TSD_DELETION

BEndonuclease-independent insertions in scope

L1 can use a pre-existing break (or dysfunctional telomere) instead of its endonuclease nick. The hallmarks disappear: no TSD, no EN motif, and the element is often truncated at both ends (no poly-A).

Reported frequency

PCAWG: 7/90 (8%) of L1-mediated deletions. Somatic rate for plain insertions is unknown.

How PEAR-TREE detects it

  • annotate: tag EN_INDEPENDENT when there is no TSD, no poly-A and both ends are truncated
  • score: few TPRT points by construction; such calls stay UNCERTAIN unless independent-fragment and colony evidence is strong

Vocabulary: tags=EN_INDEPENDENT

✕Artefacts that mimic MEIs score negative

The TPRT point system exists to separate these from the types above. None of them produces the full combination of a TSD with identity, an EN motif at the nick, a strand-consistent poly-A, concordant 5′/3′ element classes and ≥ 2 independent fragments.

Ligation, PCR and MDA chimeras

Library ligation, PCR template switching and (for single cells) whole-genome amplification join unrelated molecules. A genomic fragment fused to an L1 looks like an insertion junction. Evrony et al. showed that 7 of 13 PCR-validated candidates in an earlier single-neuron study were such chimeras: they had no TSD, could start at any L1 coordinate (including inactive or truncated copies), and 5′ and 3′ junctions from different L1s had been paired as one insertion. A-tailing during library prep can add a short poly-A at the join.

Separating it:

  • independence rule: one fragment (plus its PCR duplicates) never makes ≥ 2 independent fragments
  • CHIMERIC_ENDS when 5′ and 3′ ends come from incompatible elements
  • no TSD, no EN motif, no poly-A or only a short A-tail → few points; cross-sample identical fragments score negative

Poly-A slippage at reference A-tracts

Polymerase slippage and aligner behaviour at long reference A-tracts (typically the tails of reference Alus and L1s) produce reads soft-clipped right where the tract ends, with a clip that is simply more A. This mimics the 3′ junction of a real insertion and is the main source of discovery false positives in PEAR-TREE.

Separating it:

  • score: poly-A at both junctions or a reference A-rich context is negative
  • no element sequence beyond the poly-A, no TSD, recurrence across many unrelated loci

Mismapping among same-subfamily elements

Reads from a polymorphic or non-reference L1HS (or Alu) copy align to a near-identical reference copy and are soft-clipped where the two copies' flanks diverge. The clip is the flank of the other copy, not an insertion; a germline polymorphism can thus look somatic at the wrong locus.

Separating it:

  • clip anchored inside a reference element of the same subfamily; flank clip maps elsewhere
  • recurrence across colonies/patients and cross-sample identical fragments score negative

Fold-back palindromes

Inverted repeats in the reference and hairpin library artefacts produce reads whose clip is the reverse complement of the adjacent reference. Without element sequence, poly-A or TSD, this is an artefact; with them it may be a real fold-back inverted duplication.

Separating it:

  • score: “clip identical to the adjacent reference (fold-back)” is negative unless the clip continues into element sequence and the 3′ end has TPRT hallmarks
